# Academic Data Sharing Attitudes Survey - Help Guide
## Purpose
This survey is designed to collect information on researchers' attitudes towards academic data sharing. Your responses will help us understand the current state of data sharing practices in the academic community.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, please follow these steps:

1. Answer each question honestly and to the best of your ability.
2. Select the options that best match your experiences and opinions.
3. Make sure to review each page carefully before submitting your response.

## Field-by-Field Explanation
* **Introduction to Academic Data Sharing Survey** (`introduction_note`): This is a note section to introduce the purpose and importance of the survey.
* **What is your role in academic research?** (`researcher_role`): Please select your primary role in academic research.
	+ Principal Investigator: A researcher who initiates and leads a research project.
	+ Postdoctoral Researcher: A researcher who works after completing their doctoral degree.
	+ Graduate Student: A student who is pursuing a research degree.
	+ Undergraduate Student: A student who is pursuing an undergraduate degree.
	+ Research Staff: An employee who supports research projects.
	+ Faculty Member: A teacher or professor with a research role.
	+ Other: Select this option if your role is not listed above.
* **How many years of research experience do you have?** (`years_experience`): Please enter the number of years you have been working in research roles.
* **What is your primary research discipline?** (`discipline_area`): Please specify your main area of study.
* **How often do you share your research data with others?** (`data_sharing_frequency`): Select the option that best describes your data sharing behavior.
	+ Never: You rarely or never share your data.
	+ Rarely: You share data occasionally.
	+ Sometimes: You share data in specific situations.
	+ Often: You regularly share data.
	+ Always: You consistently share data.
* **What motivates you to share research data? (Select all that apply)** (`data_sharing_reasons`): Please choose all the reasons that apply to your data sharing behavior.
	+ Advancing scientific knowledge: You share data to advance the scientific community.
	+ Funding requirements: You share data due to funding requirements.
	+ Journal requirements: You share data to meet journal requirements.
	+ Collaboration opportunities: You share data to collaborate with others.
	+ Personal interest: You share data due to personal interest.
	+ Institutional policy: You share data due to institutional policy.
	+ None of the above: You do not have a specific reason for sharing data.
* **What barriers prevent you from sharing research data? (Select all that apply)** (`data_sharing_barriers`): Please choose all the reasons that prevent you from sharing data.
	+ Privacy concerns: You are concerned about protecting participant privacy.
	+ Intellectual property rights: You are concerned about copyright and ownership rights.
	+ Time constraints: You lack time to share data.
	+ Lack of infrastructure: You lack resources to share data.
	+ Fear of criticism: You are worried about being criticized for your work.
	+ Competition concerns: You are concerned about competing with others.
	+ Unclear policies: You are unclear about data sharing policies.
	+ Technical difficulties: You face technical challenges when sharing data.
* **How important do you think data sharing is for scientific advancement?** (`data_sharing_importance_rating`): Please rate the importance of data sharing on a scale of 1 to 5.
	+ 1: Not important
	+ 2: Slightly important
	+ 3: Somewhat important
	+ 4: Very important
	+ 5: Extremely important
* **Which data repositories do you typically use? (Select all that apply)** (`data_repository_use`): Please choose all the data repositories you use.
	+ Figshare: A data repository for sharing research data.
	+ Dryad: A data repository for sharing research data.
	+ Zenodo: A data repository for sharing research data.
	+ GitHub: A version control system for code and data.
	+ Institutional repository: A repository provided by your institution.
	+ Domain-specific repository: A repository specific to your field of study.
	+ None: You do not use any data repositories.
	+ Other: Select this option if you use another repository.
* **What type of license do you prefer for your shared data?** (`data_licensing_preference`): Please select your preferred license for shared data.
	+ Creative Commons CC BY: A license allowing others to use and share data.
	+ Creative Commons CC0: A license allowing others to use and share data without attribution.
	+ GNU GPL: A license for software and data.
	+ MIT License: A license for software and data.
	+ No license: You do not prefer any specific license.
	+ Not sure: You are unclear about licenses.
* **Do you cite data sets used in your research?** (`data_citation_practice`): Please select your citation practice for data sets.
	+ Always: You consistently cite data sources.
	+ Often: You often cite data sources.
	+ Sometimes: You sometimes cite data sources.
	+ Rarely: You rarely cite data sources.
	+ Never: You never cite data sources.
* **Have you received formal training on data sharing practices?** (`data_sharing_training`): Please select your experience with formal training on data sharing.
	+ Yes: You have received formal training.
	+ No: You have not received formal training.
	+ Not sure: You are unsure about formal training.
* **Do you create data management plans for your research projects?** (`data_management_planning`): Please select your practice for creating data management plans.
	+ Yes, always: You consistently create plans.
	+ Yes, sometimes: You sometimes create plans.
	+ No, never: You never create plans.
	+ Not sure: You are unclear about creating plans.
* **How likely are you to increase data sharing in your future research?** (`data_sharing_future_intent`): Please rate your likelihood of increasing data sharing on a scale of 1 to 5.
	+ 1: Not likely
	+ 2: Slightly likely
	+ 3: Somewhat likely
	+ 4: Very likely
	+ 5: Extremely likely
