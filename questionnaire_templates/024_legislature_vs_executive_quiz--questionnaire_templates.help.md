# Legislature Vs Executive Quiz - Help Guide
## Purpose
The "Legislature Vs Executive Quiz" form is designed to test user knowledge on the role of the legislature and the executive branch of the government. It covers essential aspects of these branches, ensuring users can distinguish between their functions and responsibilities.

## How To Complete This Form
1. Review each question carefully, and provide your response in the relevant text or selection field.
2. Ensure you've selected the correct answer for each question, as some questions have multiple options.
3. Complete all required fields, marked as "true" in the form.
4. Leave optional fields, marked as "false", as blank or select an option if desired.
5. Once finished, submit the form for evaluation.

* **What is the role of the legislature?** (`question_one`, text, required): This question asks for a brief description of the legislative branch's duties and functions. Provide a concise answer that outlines its primary responsibilities.

* **What are the powers of the executive?** (`question_two`, text, optional): Answer this question to describe the abilities and actions of the executive branch. You can mention any aspect of its powers, but note that the legislative branch has some of its powers.

### What are the branches of government?
* **What are the branches of government?** (`question_three`, select_multiple, required): Choose one or more of the options provided - House of Representatives, Senate, and Judiciary - as they represent the three branches of the US federal government.

### Who is the current president?
* **Who is the current president?** (`question_four`, select_one, true): Select the correct current president of the United States. This is a crucial piece of information, as the executive branch's head serves as the highest elected official.

### When was the government formed?
* **When was the government formed?** (`question_five`, date, optional): Answer with the date of the US government's establishment. This can be a key piece of information for historians and students of government history.
