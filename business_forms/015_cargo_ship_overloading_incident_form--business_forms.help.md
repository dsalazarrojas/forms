# Cargo Ship Overloading Incident Form - Help Guide
## Purpose
This form is designed to document and report incidents of cargo ship overloading, which is critical for ensuring the safety and integrity of cargo ship operations. Overloading can lead to significant risks, including hull stress, structural damage, and stability concerns.

## How To Complete This Form
1. Fill out the form in a step-by-step manner, ensuring that all required fields are completed accurately.
2. Start by providing the Vessel and Voyage Details, including the vessel's name, IMO number, and voyage number.
3. Proceed to the Incident Summary section to record a brief description of the incident.
4. Next, provide the Incident Details, including the date, time, location, and actual draft at departure.
5. In the Impact and Actions section, assess the impact on the vessel's stability and identify the risk of structural damage.
6. Document the immediate actions taken to address the overloading incident.
7. Finally, confirm the incident report's accuracy and compliance with regulatory requirements.

## Field-by-Field Explanation

* **Vessel Name**: The name of the cargo ship involved in the incident.
* **IMO Number**: The unique identification number for the ship, as per international maritime regulations.
* **Voyage Number**: The current journey identifier for the vessel.
* **Master's Name**: The name of the commanding officer on board the vessel.
* **Date of Incident**: The date when the overloading incident occurred.
* **Time of Incident**: The time when the overloading incident occurred.
* **Current Location**: The location where the incident took place, using latitude and longitude coordinates or a port name.
* **Maximum Permissible Draft (m)**: The maximum draft allowed for the vessel, as specified in the load line certificate.
* **Actual Draft at Departure (m)**: The actual draft reading at the time of departure.
* **Observed Over-draft (cm)**: The difference between the actual and permissible draft.
* **Load Line Status**: A physical observation of the load line, with options Above Line, Below Line, and Submerged.
* **Primary Cause of Overloading**: A selection of the primary reason for the overloading incident, from Calculation Error, Incorrect Cargo Weight, Unauthorized Cargo, Water Ingress, Improper Ballasting, or Other.
* **Provide details on the cause**: A text description of how the overloading discrepancy occurred.
* **Impact on Vessel Stability**: An assessment of the incident's impact on the vessel's stability, with options Negligible, Minimal, Significant, or Dangerous.
* **Observed Risk of Structural Damage**: A selection of whether there's a risk of structural damage, with options True, False, or Possible.
* **Immediate Actions Taken**: A description of the actions taken to address the overloading incident.
* **Port Authority Notified**: A selection of whether the local port authority was notified, with options True or False.
* **I confirm this incident report is accurate**: A digital signature confirming the report's accuracy.
