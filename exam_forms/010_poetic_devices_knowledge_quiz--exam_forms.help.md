# Poetic Devices Knowledge Quiz - Help Guide
## Purpose
This form is designed to test your knowledge of poetic devices, a literary technique used to convey meaning and create vivid descriptions in poetry and literature.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, simply select the answers to each question based on your understanding of the poetic devices. You can choose one or multiple options for each question, as indicated by the type of each field.

## Field-by-Field Explanation
- **What is poetic devices** (`what_is_poetic_devices`, text, required): This field is not actually present in the form, so it's not necessary to worry about it. Please start with the next question.
- **What is a simile** (`what_is_a_simile`, select_one, required): A simile is a figure of speech that compares two things using the words "like" or "as". For example, "he runs like a cheetah".
- **What is a metaphor** (`what_is_a_metaphor`, select_multiple, required): A metaphor is a figure of speech that directly compares two things without using "like" or "as". For example, "he is a lion".
- **What is hyperbole** (`what_is_hyperbole`, text, required): Hyperbole is a figure of speech that involves an exaggeration used for emphasis or effect. For example, "I'm so hungry I could eat a whole elephant".
- **What is personification** (`what_is_personification`, select_one, required): Personification is a figure of speech that gives human-like qualities to non-human things. For example, "the sun smiled at me".
- **What is alliteration** (`what_is_alliteration`, text, required): Alliteration is a figure of speech that involves the repetition of initial consonant sounds. For example, "she sells seashells".
- **What is assonance** (`what_is_asonance`, text, required): Assonance is a figure of speech that involves the repetition of vowel sounds. For example, "the u sound in 'cute' is an example of assonance".
- **What is consonance** (`what_is_consonance`, text, required): Consonance is a figure of speech that involves the repetition of consonant sounds. For example, "the c-k sounds in 'lock and key' is an example of consonance".
