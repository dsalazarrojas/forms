# Hoarding Support Assessment - Help Guide
## Purpose
The Hoarding Support Assessment form is a comprehensive document that helps healthcare professionals or support workers collect detailed information about an individual's or organization's hoarding behavior, medical and mental history, and other relevant factors. This information will be used to create an effective support plan tailored to the client's unique needs.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, follow these steps:

*   Review each section carefully to understand what information is being asked.
*   Answer all the questions honestly and thoroughly.
*   Use the provided fields to enter your responses accurately.
*   If you're unsure about a particular question, please don't hesitate to seek clarification or guidance from your support worker or healthcare professional.

## Field-by-Field Explanation

*   **Introduction** (Page 1): This is an open-ended section for you to introduce yourself or the client, providing any relevant context or information about the client's situation or condition.
*   **Client Info** (Page 2): Enter the client's basic demographic information such as name, age, or contact details.
*   **Hoarding Patterns** (Page 3): Describe the client's hoarding patterns, habits, or behaviors related to hoarding. Provide specific examples or incidents if possible.
*   **Client History** (Page 4): Outline the client's history of medical conditions, mental health issues, or other relevant personal details that might impact their hoarding behavior or overall well-being.
*   **Social History** (Page 5): Describe the client's social interactions, relationships, or community engagement relevant to their hoarding patterns or behavior.
*   **Mental Health** (Page 6): Share the client's mental health status, including any psychological disorders, conditions, or treatments that may be relevant.
*   **Medical History** (Page 7): Provide a detailed medical history of the client, including any chronic or acute conditions, treatments, or hospitalizations.
*   **Family History** (Page 8): Outline the client's family history, including any genetic conditions, family dynamics, or relevant social interactions.
*   **Medical Conditions** (Page 9): Indicate if the client has any medical conditions or disabilities that impact their daily life or hoarding behavior.
*   **Medications** (Page 10): List any medications the client is currently taking or has taken in the past that might be relevant to their hoarding behavior or condition.
*   **Emergency Contacts** (Page 11): Provide the client's emergency contact information, including name, relationship, and contact details.
*   **Next Steps** (Page 12): Outline the next steps you plan to take in supporting the client or addressing their hoarding behavior.
*   **Additional Comments** (Page 13): Add any additional information or observations about the client that might be relevant to their support or care plan.
*   **Date Last Contact** (Page 14): Enter the date you last contacted the client.
*   **Time Last Contact** (Page 15): Enter the time of your last contact with the client.
*   **Date Last Update** (Page 16): Enter the date this assessment was last updated.
*   **Time Last Update** (Page 17): Enter the time this assessment was last updated.
*   **Date Completed** (Page 18): Enter the date this assessment was completed.
*   **Time Completed** (Page 19): Enter the time this assessment was completed.
*   **Submitted By** (Page 20): Enter your contact information as the person submitting this assessment.
*   **Submitted On** (Page 21): Enter the date this assessment was submitted.
*   **Submitted At** (Page 22): Enter the time this assessment was submitted.
*   **Created By** (Page 23): Enter your contact information as the person creating this assessment.
*   **Created On** (Page 24): Enter the date this assessment was created.
*   **Created At** (Page 25): Enter the time this assessment was created.

Please remember to fill out all required fields accurately and thoroughly. If you have any questions or concerns, don't hesitate to seek guidance from your support worker or healthcare professional.
