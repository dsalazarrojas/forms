# Falls Risk Assessment Survey - Help Guide
## Purpose
This survey is used to assess the risk of falls in patients, including identifying risk factors and determining the level of risk.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, please follow these steps:
1. Select the appropriate risk assessment level for the patient (e.g., High, Medium, or Low).
2. List any medications that may be contributing to the patient's risk of falls (Sedatives, Antidepressants, Anti-anxiety).
3. Identify any risk factors present in the patient (Age, Dizziness, Confusion, History, Medication).
4. Enter the date and time of the assessment.
5. Indicate who conducted the assessment.

## Field-by-Field Explanation
- **Falls Risk Assessment** (Text, Optional): Describe the patient's falls risk assessment, including any factors that were considered.
  - **Patient Falls Risk** (Number, Optional): Enter a numerical value to indicate the patient's falls risk level.
  - **Assess Falls Risk** (Select, Optional): Select the level of risk associated with falls (High, Medium, Low).
  - **Assess Risk Factors** (Multi-select, Optional): Identify any risk factors present, such as Age, Dizziness, Confusion, History, or Medication.
  - **Medication Side Effects** (Multi-select, Optional): Select any medication side effects contributing to the patient's risk of falls (Sedatives, Antidepressants, Anti-anxiety).
  - **Patient Medication List** (Text, Optional): Provide a list of the patient's current medications.
  - **Fall Risk Level** (Select, Optional): Select the overall fall risk level of the patient (Low, Medium, High).
  - **Assessment Date** (Date, Optional): Enter the date of the assessment.
  - **Assessment Time** (Time, Optional): Enter the time of the assessment.
  - **Assessment By** (Text, Optional): Indicate who conducted the assessment.

## Tips
- Please answer truthfully and thoroughly to ensure accurate results.
- If you are unsure of a particular risk factor, please select "Unknown".
- If you have any questions or concerns, please consult with a healthcare professional.
