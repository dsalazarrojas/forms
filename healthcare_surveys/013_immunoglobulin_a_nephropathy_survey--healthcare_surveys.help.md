# Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy Survey - Help Guide
## Purpose
The Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy Survey is a form designed to collect data on symptoms, quality of life, support needs, and healthcare access of individuals suspected or diagnosed with immunoglobulin A nephropathy. This information will be used to help medical professionals better understand the condition and provide more effective treatment.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, follow these steps:

1. Answer each question accurately and honestly.
2. Please note that some questions have multiple choice options, select the one that best describes your situation.
3. Enter numerical values for questions that require them.
4. Be as detailed as possible when answering open-ended questions.
5. If you are unsure about any question, please consult your healthcare provider for guidance.

## Field-by-Field Explanation

* **Symptoms** (1): Describe any symptoms you are experiencing, such as fatigue, swelling, or pain.
* **Quality of Life** (2): Describe how your condition has affected your quality of life, using a scale of 1 (very poor) to 5 (excellent).
* **Support Needs** (3): Select all the support systems you currently rely on, such as family, friends, or support groups.
* **Healthcare Access** (4): Indicate whether you have access to healthcare services, such as doctors or hospitals.
* **Healthcare Experience** (5): Rate your experience with healthcare services, using a scale of 1 (poor) to 4 (excellent).
* **Symptoms Duration** (6): Enter the duration of your symptoms in months.
* **Symptoms Frequency** (7): Enter how frequently you experience symptoms, using a scale of 1 (daily) to 6 (rarely).
* **Symptoms Severity** (8): Rate the severity of your symptoms, using a scale of 1 (mild) to 5 (severe).
* **Quality of Life Scale** (9): Rate your quality of life, using a scale of 1 (very poor) to 5 (excellent).
* **Healthcare Services Utilization** (10): Select all healthcare services you have used or plan to use, such as doctor visits or hospitalizations.
* **Healthcare Access Barriers** (11): Describe any barriers you face when accessing healthcare services.
* **Healthcare Access Barriers Frequency** (12): Enter how frequently you experience barriers to healthcare access, using a scale of 1 (daily) to 6 (rarely).
* **Healthcare Access Barriers Severity** (13): Rate the severity of barriers to healthcare access, using a scale of 1 (mild) to 5 (severe).
* **Healthcare Access Barriers Duration** (14): Enter the duration of barriers to healthcare access, in months.
* **Support Needs** (15): Select all support systems you rely on, such as family, friends, or support groups.
* **Support Needs Frequency** (16): Enter how frequently you rely on support systems, using a scale of 1 (daily) to 6 (rarely).
* **Support Needs Severity** (17): Rate the severity of support needs, using a scale of 1 (mild) to 5 (severe).
* **Quality of Life Affected** (18): Rate how your quality of life has been affected by your condition, using a scale of 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much).
* **Quality of Life Improvement** (19): Rate the improvement in your quality of life, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
* **Symptoms Affected** (20): Rate how your symptoms have been affected by your condition, using a scale of 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much).
* **Symptoms Improvement** (21): Rate the improvement in your symptoms, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
* **Support Needs Improvement** (22): Rate the improvement in your support needs, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
* **Healthcare Access Improvement** (23): Rate the improvement in healthcare access, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
* **Quality of Life Scale Improvement** (24): Rate the improvement in quality of life scale, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
* **Symptoms Severity Improvement** (25): Rate the improvement in symptoms severity, using a scale of 1 (worse) to 5 (much better).
