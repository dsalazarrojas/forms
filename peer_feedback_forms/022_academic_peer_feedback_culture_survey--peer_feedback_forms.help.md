# Academic Peer Feedback Culture Survey - Help Guide
## Purpose
The Academic Peer Feedback Culture Survey aims to understand the perceptions of academic peer feedback within your institution. This form will help gather information on how often you give or receive feedback, your comfort levels with giving and receiving feedback, and how you perceive the quality and culture of peer feedback.

## How To Complete This Form

1.  Please answer the questions honestly, and select only the options that apply to you.
2.  For questions with scales, use the provided numbers to rate your answers (e.g., 1-5).
3.  For questions with multiple select options, choose all that apply.

## Field-by-Field Explanation

### **1. Academic Peer Feedback Culture Survey**

*   This is the title of the form and serves as a header.

### **2. Your Role in the Academic Community**

*   Select the option that best describes your primary role: Faculty member, Graduate student, Undergraduate student, Administrator, Staff member, Postdoctoral researcher, or Other.

### **3. Years of Experience**

*   Enter the number of years you have spent in academia.

### **4. Department or College**

*   Enter your primary academic department or college.

### **5. Peer Feedback Frequency**

*   Select how often you give or receive peer feedback: Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Annually, Rarely, or Never.

### **6. Comfort with Giving Feedback**

*   Rate your comfort level with giving peer feedback on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means least comfortable and 5 means most comfortable.

### **7. Comfort with Receiving Feedback**

*   Rate your comfort level with receiving peer feedback on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means least comfortable and 5 means most comfortable.

### **8. Feedback Quality Rating**

*   Rate the quality of peer feedback in your institution on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means lowest quality and 5 means highest quality.

### **9. Feedback Culture Rating**

*   Rate the peer feedback culture in your institution on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means lowest quality and 5 means highest quality.

### **10. Perceived Benefits of Peer Feedback**

*   Select all benefits you see in peer feedback: Improved teaching methods, Enhanced research collaboration, Professional development, Increased accountability, Better communication, Reduced isolation, Quality improvement, or None.

### **11. Perceived Challenges of Peer Feedback**

*   Select all challenges you see in peer feedback: Time constraints, Lack of training, Power dynamics, Defensiveness, Lack of trust, Unclear guidelines, Fear of retaliation, or None.

### **12. Preferred Feedback Format**

*   Select the format you prefer for peer feedback: Formal written reports, Informal conversations, Structured meetings, Online surveys, Peer observation, Group discussions, or Other.

### **13. Feedback Anonymity Preference**

*   Select whether you prefer feedback to be anonymous or attributed: Anonymous feedback, Attributed feedback, Either is fine, Depends on context.

### **14. Feedback Training Received**

*   Select whether you have received training on giving or receiving peer feedback: Yes, extensive, Yes, some, No, but interested, or No, and not interested.

### **15. Clarity of Feedback Guidelines**

*   Rate the clarity of guidelines for peer feedback on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means least clear and 5 means most clear.

### **16. Impact on Performance**

*   Rate the impact of peer feedback on your academic performance on a scale of 1-5, where 1 means least impact and 5 means greatest impact.

### **17. Suggestions for Improvement**

*   Provide any suggestions you have to improve the peer feedback culture at your institution.
