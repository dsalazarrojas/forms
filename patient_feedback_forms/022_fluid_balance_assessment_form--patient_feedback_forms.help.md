# Fluid Balance Assessment Form - Help Guide
## Purpose
The Fluid Balance Assessment Form is used to assess a patient's current fluid balance status. This form helps healthcare professionals monitor and manage fluid balance issues, ensuring timely interventions and better patient outcomes.

## How To Complete This Form

1. **Fluid Intake**: Enter the total amount of fluid the patient has taken in, in units of mL (milliliters). This is the total amount of fluid from all sources, including medications, food, and drinks.
2. **Fluid Output**: Enter the total amount of fluid the patient has lost, in units of mL (milliliters). This includes urine output, stool output, and any other fluid loss from the patient's body.
3. **Total Fluid Balance**: This is calculated automatically as the difference between Fluid Intake and Fluid Output.
4. **Comments**: Add any additional notes or information about the patient's fluid balance status. This could include comments from previous assessments or any observations from the care team.
5. **Fluid Balance Form Complete**: Mark this field if the form is complete and accurate.
6. **Date**: Enter the date and time of the assessment, ensuring the most up-to-date information is captured.
7. **Time**: Enter the time of the assessment.
8. **Completed By**: Indicate whether the form was completed by the patient or the assessor (e.g., nurse, doctor, or other healthcare professional).
9. **Comments**: Add any additional comments from the assessor, e.g., "I reviewed the form and confirmed its accuracy."
10. **Assessor**: Identify who completed the form.
11. **Status**: Mark the patient's status as Active or Inactive, indicating whether they are currently receiving care.
12. **Review Comments**: Add any comments or observations from the reviewer.
13. **Review By**: Indicate whether the form was reviewed by a healthcare professional.
14. **Fluid Balance Form Complete**: Mark this field if the form is accurate and complete.

Note: This form is not intended for duplicate submissions. Each submission should represent a new assessment of the patient's fluid balance status.
