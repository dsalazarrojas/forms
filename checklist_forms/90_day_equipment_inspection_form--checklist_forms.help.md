# 90 Day Equipment Inspection Form - Help Guide
## Purpose

This form is intended to aid in the systematic assessment and monitoring of equipment condition over a 90-day period. It collects critical information to facilitate timely and effective maintenance, inspection, and decision-making processes within your organization.

## How To Complete This Form

1.  Fill out the form with the provided sections and fields in the following order.
2.  Enter your name as the inspector in the "Inspector name" field.
3.  Enter the equipment's ID or serial number in the "Equipment ID or serial number" field.
4.  Select the type of equipment from the provided options in the "Equipment type" field.
5.  Enter the date of the inspection in the "Inspection date" field.
6.  Provide the operating hours or odometer reading for the equipment at the time of inspection.
7.  Assess the visual condition of the equipment, checking for any signs of damage or deterioration.
8.  Indicate whether safety equipment is present and functional for the selected options.
9.  Check the lubrication and fluid levels against recommended ranges.
10.  Verify that the electrical system is functional (battery, wiring, lights).
11.  Evaluate the condition of the tires and brakes.
12.  Check for any hydraulic leaks.
13.  Perform an operational test to check if the equipment operates normally or if there are any issues.
14.  List any defects or issues observed during the inspection.
15.  Provide any additional notes or recommended actions for repairs or maintenance.
16.  Suggest a recommended next inspection date if applicable.
17.  Finally, sign with your name as the inspector, confirming your electronic signature.


## Field-by-Field Explanation

*   **Inspector name**: Print your full name as the inspector conducting the form. This ensures accountability for the findings and actions taken or recommended. It's an important part of maintaining a record and allowing others to identify who conducted the inspection.
*   **Equipment ID or serial number**: Enter the unique identifier for the equipment being inspected or the VIN for vehicles. This helps track the individual equipment item and ensures that all inspections are specific and accurate.
*   **Equipment type**: Choose the best match from the provided options (Truck / Vehicle, Generator, Heavy machinery / Excavator, Forklift, Trailer, Other). Select the type that most accurately describes the equipment being inspected.
*   **Inspection date**: Enter the date the inspection was conducted. This is crucial for scheduling follow-up inspections and for tracking the maintenance and operation history of the equipment.
*   **Operating hours or odometer reading**: Record the current operating hours or odometer reading for the equipment. This data helps in understanding the usage and condition over time.
*   **Visual condition assessment**: Perform a quick external check for dents, corrosion, or other signs of damage or deterioration. This helps in identifying potential issues before they become major problems.
*   **Safety equipment present and functional**: Indicate whether safety equipment such as fire extinguishers, first aid kits, warning lights, seat belts, and safety guards are present and in good working condition. List all that apply.
*   **Lubrication and fluid levels check**: Check that the fluid levels are within recommended ranges for the equipment. This is crucial for maintaining the equipment's condition and longevity.
*   **Electrical system functional check**: Verify that the battery, wiring, and lights are functioning correctly. This affects both the operation and safety of the equipment.
*   **Tires and brakes condition**: Check for tread condition, pressure, and brake response. This is critical for ensuring safe operation and preventing accidents.
*   **Hydraulic leaks observed**: Check for any leaks in the hydraulic system. This can indicate potential problems with the system's performance or longevity.
*   **Operational test result**: Perform a basic operational test to see if the equipment operates normally, has intermittent issues, or fails to operate at all.
*   **Defects or issues found**: List any defects or issues observed during the inspection. This could include fluid leaks, damaged hoses or belts, electrical faults, brake issues, or structural damage.
*   **Inspector notes and recommended actions**: Provide any additional notes or recommended actions for repairs, maintenance, or further inspections. This helps in planning and executing necessary actions.
*   **Recommended next inspection date**: Suggest a date for the next inspection if applicable. This helps in maintaining a regular inspection schedule and ensuring timely maintenance.
*   **Inspector signature (type name)**: Sign with your name as the inspector, providing an electronic signature that confirms you've completed the form accurately and truthfully.

## Tips

- Always perform the inspections according to the provided guidelines and your organization's maintenance schedule.
- Ensure that all fields are filled accurately and completely.
- Use this form consistently to maintain a thorough and organized record of your inspections.
- If unsure about any aspect of the equipment or the inspection process, seek guidance from a relevant expert or follow your organization's standard operating procedures.
- Keep all records of filled-out forms in a secure and easily accessible location for future reference and review.
