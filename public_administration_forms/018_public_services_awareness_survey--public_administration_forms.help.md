# Public Services Awareness Survey - Help Guide
## Purpose
The Public Services Awareness Survey is a tool used by public administrators to gauge the awareness and satisfaction of public services among the population.

## How To Complete This Form
To complete this form, follow these steps:

1. Read each question carefully and answer honestly.
2. For questions 1-3, type a brief response in the text field.
3. For questions 4 and 5, select one or more options that apply to you.
4. If you have any other challenges in accessing public services, please describe them in question 6.
5. For question 7, select a time that applies to you.

## Field-by-Field Explanation

* **Awareness (1)** (`awareness`, text, optional): This question asks about your current level of awareness of public services. Please type a brief response, such as "I am aware of most public services" or "I am not aware of any public services".
* **Usage (2)** (`usage`, text, optional): This question asks about your usage of public services. Please type a brief response, such as "I use public services frequently" or "I rarely use public services".
* **Satisfaction (3)** (`satisfaction`, text, optional): This question asks about your satisfaction with public services. Please type a brief response, such as "I am very satisfied" or "I am not satisfied at all".
* **Region (4)** (`region`, select_one, required): This question asks you to select the region where you live. Please choose between "urban", "rural", or "suburban".
* **Access Challenges (5)** (`access-challenges`, select_multiple, required): This question asks you to select one or more challenges you face in accessing public services. Please choose from the options: "cost", "time constraints", "lack of information", or "distance".
* **Other Access Challenges (6)** (`other-access-challenges`, text, optional): If you face any other challenges not listed, please describe them in this text field.
* **Time (7)** (`time`, time, required): This question asks you to select a time that applies to you, such as "morning", "afternoon", or "evening".
